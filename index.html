About

My work sits between carbohydrate chemistry and biomimetic catalysis. I use glycals and their rearrangements to reach pharmacologically relevant scaffolds, and I design copper–ligand systems that imitate the way enzymes activate inert bonds.

At Pondicherry University, under Prof. J. Kandasamy, I developed a synthetic route from D-glucal to D-rhamnal and contributed experimentally to Pd-catalysed C-2 arylation of 2,3-pseudoglycals, synthesising and characterising 15+ pyranoside derivatives. My Ferrier Type-I studies gave 2,3-unsaturated glycosides in yields above 95% across four glycal precursors.

I am now a Junior Research Fellow at NIT Calicut under Dr. M. Sankaralingam, developing copper-based functional models of lytic polysaccharide monooxygenases (LPMOs) for biomass valorisation. I am seeking a European PhD in synthetic methodology, green catalysis and chemical biology.

Research experience

Each project as a scientific narrative: the question, my contribution, and what emerged.

Nature-inspired Conversion of Biomass to Biofuel: The LPMO Paradigm

Project Associate (JRF) · NIT Calicut, India · Feb 2026 – present · Advisor: Dr. M. Sankaralingam

Can synthetic molecular systems and functional nanomaterials replicate the oxidative C–H activation that copper-dependent LPMOs achieve in nature?

I designed and characterised tetradentate Cu complexes with systematically varied ligand environments as functional models of LPMOs for polysaccharide oxidation, and studied how ligand denticity, sterics and electronics affect catalyst behaviour.

To test the role of supports, I synthesised MIL-100 MOFs and ferrites. Molecular catalysts are compared on p-nitrophenyl glycoside model substrates by UV–Vis spectroscopy, which guides later work with natural polysaccharides. Mechanistic studies by DFT and analytical methods are in progress.

  • Ligand design
  • Cu coordination chemistry
  • Cyclic voltammetry
  • MOFs and ferrites
  • Biomass valorisation
  • DFT

Ferrier Rearrangements, D-Rhamnal Synthesis and C-2 Aryl Glycosides

Graduate Researcher (M.Sc. thesis and internship) · Pondicherry University, India · July 2024 – May 2025 · Advisor: Prof. Dr. J. Kandasamy

Can a systematic Ferrier-I methodology serve as the entry to an efficient D-rhamnal synthesis, and can that rhamnal be functionalised by Pd-catalysed C-2 arylation?

Internship, July–Dec 2024. I optimised the Ferrier Type-I rearrangement across four glycal precursors (D-glucal, D-galactal, D-rhamnal, L-rhamnal) and evaluated catalytic approaches, with yields above 95%. A critical review of 25+ publications compared classical BF₃·OEt₂ conditions with ionic liquids, Amberlyst, zeolites and photocatalytic systems on atom economy, E-factor, selectivity and scalability.

Thesis, Jan–May 2025. I developed a route from D-glucal to D-rhamnal through selective C-6 modification and C-5 configuration control, shorter than existing literature methods. As part of this training I contributed experimentally to the work underlying the methodology reported in J. Org. Chem. 2026, 91, 9028–9044 (Pd-catalysed C-2 arylation of 2,3-pseudoglycals), synthesising and characterising 15+ pyranoside derivatives and interpreting their spectra.

The scope study showed two limits: anomeric selectivity varies with the glycal and aryl partner, and some protecting-group combinations suppress reactivity or cause elimination and migration. These define the questions I want to pursue in a PhD.

  • Ferrier Type-I rearrangement
  • Pd-catalysed C–C coupling
  • Selective C-6 deoxygenation
  • Protecting-group strategy
  • Anomeric selectivity
  • C-glycosides
  • NMR, ESI-HRMS

Research vision

The problems I want to solve in a PhD.

Carbohydrates remain hard to access synthetically: selective C-glycosidic bond formation, with control of anomeric configuration and C-2 functionality, still needs long routes that do not suit analogue synthesis in drug discovery. I believe C–H and C–M activation strategies on glycal platforms offer unexplored solutions.

LPMOs show that nature activates inert C–H bonds in polysaccharides with copper coordination chemistry. My ambition is to design biomimetic Cu catalysts that combine the precision of the LPMO active site with the accessibility needed for scalable biomass valorisation.
